    What this demo guarantees, and what it does not

    • Fewer guardians than agreed learn nothing about the secret, not even with all the computing power in the world (Shamir secret sharing).
    • The secret never leaves the device of whoever splits or recovers it. In the links, the share comes after the #, which the browser sends to no server, not even ours.
    • Altered shares are noticed: before splitting, the secret is wrapped so that any change to a share scrambles the whole result, and the page notices with just the agreed number of shares. With one share more, it finds and discards the wrong one. This check only exists with all the needed shares together: no single share carries a digest of the secret, which would let a guardian try to guess a weak password.
    • The weak point is sending: sent messages stay on your phone and in backups. Delete them, use different channels, or hand the shares over in person with the QR code.
    • Nothing is stored on a server: if too many guardians lose their shares, so that fewer than the agreed number remain, nobody can recover the secret, not even us.

    Format LS2 · the secret is padded to a multiple of 32 bytes (so its exact length is not revealed), gets 16 zero bytes for checking and is wrapped OAEP-style (G and H: MGF1 with SHA-256); the package is split with Shamir over GF(28), byte by byte. The share header (name of the secret, guardians’ names etc.) goes into the hash, so it is checked too. Extra wrong shares are found with Berlekamp–Welch or by searching subsets. Each share carries a CRC-32 against copying errors (not a signature). After the share, each link carries a short hint of the format (&h=…), so a programmer can recover the secret even without this page. QR generator: qrcode-generator, by Kazuhiko Arase (MIT license).
